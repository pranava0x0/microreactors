## The five findings most worth surfacing
1. **Nobody has removed their diesel.** In every dated precedent found, the alternative takes
   the run hours and diesel is demoted to secondary. Kaiser Ontario's 9 MWh buys **10 hours**.
2. **Surplus interconnection is real and accelerating** — 35 executed agreements naming a
   counterparty, 1 → 3 → 3 → 9 → **19** (2022→2026 to August). Zero involve a reactor.
3. **The "a microreactor sits under the interconnection thresholds" claim is false.** FERC's
   pro forma SGIP caps *synchronous* machines at **2 MW** for Fast Track; the 3–5 MW rows are
   certified inverter-based only. A 2–20 MW reactor gets the full Study Process.
4. **Civic critical facilities are an order of magnitude too small.** Montgomery County's jail
   CHP is 220 kW and its Public Safety HQ 800 kW. Airports (Pittsburgh ~14 MW) and medical
   centres (TECO/TMC 48 MW) are the right-sized civic buyers.
5. **Price to beat, by sector:** rural Alaska $0.41–$1.95/kWh (regulator-certified);
   northern industrial self-generation $0.25–$0.60/kWh; healthcare **$0.15/kWh flat for 30
   years** (Loma Linda) against SCE retail of $0.17–0.24; marine terminals **$7.0M per
   container berth** (CARB) and $14.7M per cruise berth (Seattle).
