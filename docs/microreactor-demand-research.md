# Microreactor demand-side evidence base — for an Antares R1 commercial-deployment pitch

Captured 2026-08-18. Built for a commercial-deployment pitch to **Antares** (LA-area microreactor company; product **R1**, 100 kWe–1 MWe class, TRISO-fueled, 6+ years between refuelings, truck-delivered/factory-produced; first delivery targeted at Joint Base San Antonio, 2028). See `docs/specs/antares-facts.md` for the company/product/federal fact base this document assumes and does not repeat.

Six sections, one per demand angle, each dated to its own announcement/publication date (not today's capture date) unless noted. URLs are copied verbatim from fetches/search results — none reconstructed.

---

## 1. Hyperscalers with a nuclear scale-out strategy already

The 2024–2026 wave, chronological, each with buyer, seller, size, and instrument:

- **2024-09-20 — Microsoft + Constellation Energy.** 20-year power purchase agreement (PPA) for the full output of a restarted Three Mile Island Unit 1, renamed the **Crane Clean Energy Center** (Middletown, PA), 835 MW. Constellation is investing roughly $1.6B in the restart; NRC approval required; restart timing reported as targeted for 2027, accelerated from an original 2028 estimate. [Constellation Energy; Utility Dive; CNBC — 2024-09-20]
- **2024-10-14 — Google + Kairos Power.** Corporate PPA ("Master Plant Development Agreement") for a fleet of small modular reactors (SMRs): first unit online by 2030, ramping to 500 MW of capacity by 2035. Google's own framing: "the world's first corporate agreement to purchase nuclear energy from multiple small modular reactors." [Google, blog.google, 2024-10-14]
- **October 2024 — Amazon, three-part announcement.** (1) Equity investment in X-energy plus an agreement supporting more than 5 GW of Xe-100 projects across the U.S. through 2039; (2) a Carbon Free Development and Funding Agreement with **Energy Northwest** (Washington state) for four Xe-100 reactors, initial 320 MWe with an option to upsize to 960 MWe, online "early 2030s"; (3) an SMR feasibility agreement with **Dominion Energy** near the North Anna station (Virginia), "at least 300 MW." A separate, earlier Amazon deal (March 2024) had Amazon buy a data-center campus co-located with Talen Energy's Susquehanna nuclear plant (PA) — a site-power deal, not a new-build PPA, and one that drew FERC/grid-interconnection scrutiny. [aboutamazon.com; Utility Dive]
- **2025-06-03 — Meta + Constellation Energy.** 20-year PPA for the entire output of the **Clinton Clean Energy Center** (IL), a single 1,121 MW reactor; deliveries begin June 2027. Constellation is also evaluating an SMR or new-reactor addition at the same site. [Constellation Energy, 2025-06-03; CNBC]
- **December 2024 — Meta's SMR RFP.** Meta issued a request for proposals to nuclear developers seeking **1–4 GW of new U.S. advanced-nuclear generation** — Meta's first formal nuclear move, six months before the Clinton PPA. [reported via ESG Dive / Utility Dive coverage of the Dec-2024 RFP]
- **September 2024 — Oracle: design intent, not a signed deal.** Larry Ellison stated on an earnings call that Oracle was designing a data center powered by three small modular reactors. Every source checked here treats this as a stated design intention, not a named SMR-vendor PPA — flag as a weaker instrument than the others on this list.
- **Announced September 2025 — Oracle/OpenAI "Stargate" compute deal.** Reported as $300B for roughly 4.5 GW of compute capacity, deliveries starting 2027 (Wall Street Journal, via Techmeme). This is a compute-purchase agreement, not a nuclear PPA — the underlying power mix isn't specified in this reporting — included only as context for the capacity wave, not as a nuclear data point.
- **OpenAI-adjacent: Sam Altman and Oklo.** Altman chaired Oklo's board and remains a major shareholder; he stepped down as chair in **April 2025** specifically over a conflict of interest — OpenAI itself may want to buy power from Oklo, which can't happen with Altman on both boards. Oklo targets first commercial operation of its Aurora-INL reactor **late 2027/2028**. No signed OpenAI-Oklo PPA surfaced in this research — a governance signal, not a deal. [Newsweek; The Motley Fool; DataCenterDynamics]
- **xAI: checked, nothing nuclear found.** xAI's Memphis/Southaven, MS build-out (the "Colossus" cluster) runs on gas turbines in every source surfaced here. No xAI-nuclear deal exists as of this research (2026-08-18).

**Pitch's use of this section:** every hyperscaler above has already cleared the internal bar for buying nuclear power — board sign-off, legal review of a novel PPA structure, sustainability-team buy-in. For any of these companies (or a peer not yet on this list), a 1 MW-class resilience layer is a second nuclear purchase riding on cleared internal precedent, not a first one requiring new organizational appetite.

## 2. Equinix deep-dive

The single most important comparable for this pitch: Equinix is the one hyperscaler-adjacent buyer that has already purchased microreactors, not just utility-scale SMRs.

**2025-08-14 — Equinix's bundled announcement** (https://investor.equinix.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1079/equinix-collaborates-with-leading-alternative-energy; mirrored at newsroom.equinix.com and prnewswire.com), four deals plus a fuel-cell expansion:

- **Radiant — 20 Kaleidos microreactors, preorder with deposits paid.** Each Kaleidos is rated **1.2 MWe / 3 MWth** — in the same power class as Antares's R1 (100 kWe–1 MWe) — a high-temperature gas-cooled reactor using TRISO fuel, helium coolant, and prismatic graphite blocks; Radiant markets it as transportable and "installed in days." At release time Radiant had completed front-end engineering and experiment design for a Kaleidos prototype, targeting lab testing as early as mid-2026. [World Nuclear News; DataCenterDynamics; Radiant's own release via Yahoo Finance/Newswire]
- **Oklo — up to 500 MW, originally April 2024.** Predates the August 2025 bundle: Equinix signed the deal (revealed via an SEC S-4 filing dated 2024-04-02) with a $25M prepayment, becoming "the first data center operator to sign an agreement with an SMR company." Oklo's Aurora powerhouses run on recycled nuclear fuel. [DataCenterDynamics, 2024-04; NucNet, 2024-04-02]
- **ULC-Energy + Rolls-Royce SMR — Letter of Intent for a PPA up to 250 MWe, Netherlands.** ULC-Energy would build using Rolls-Royce's 470 MWe light-water SMR design; the LOI, not yet a binding PPA, targets Dutch data centers.
- **Stellaria — pre-order power agreement, 500 MWe, Europe.** Stellaria's design is billed as "the very first molten salt Breed & Burn reactor in the world"; Equinix holds the first capacity reservation on Stellaria's planned reactor ("the Stellarium"), targeting deployment around 2035.
- **Bloom Energy fuel cells — expansion to 100+ MW across 19 data centers in 6 states**, a 10-plus-year deployment track record, claimed to avoid 285,000 MTCO2e and 382 billion gallons of embedded water use.
- **Combined framing:** press headlines split the number two ways and both check out arithmetically — DCD's "more than 750MW" (774 MWe) covers just the three *new* Aug-2025 deals (Radiant 24 MWe + ULC 250 MWe + Stellaria 500 MWe); adding the pre-existing Oklo 500 MW brings the total past **1.2 GW** of contracted/pre-ordered advanced-nuclear capacity, cited elsewhere as "1,250MW." [StockTitan; DCD]
- **Strategy quote, Raouf Abdel (EVP, Global Operations — owns Design, Construction, Procurement, Energy, and IBX Operations; ex-President/CEO, OpTerra Energy Group):** "Access to round-the-clock electricity is critical to support the infrastructure that powers everything from AI-driven drug discovery to cloud-based video streaming." Equinix frames this as a **diversified portfolio** — utility partnerships, on-site generation (fuel cells, gas), transmission upgrades, and advanced nuclear — against a stated 2030 goal of 100% clean/renewable energy (96% globally as of the release).

**2026 update search: nothing new found.** Two separate searches — a general "2026 update" pass and a source-restricted pass against World Nuclear News/DataCenterDynamics/Reuters — surfaced no Equinix nuclear or fuel-cell deal *after* 2025-08-14 in a reliable outlet. One AI-aggregator site (enkiai.com) claimed Equinix "finalized PPAs" in "early 2026" with different MW figures (200+/150/150+ for Stellaria/Radiant/Oklo respectively) — those numbers contradict the primary release's own figures (500/24/500 MWe) and are not used here; flagged as a likely aggregation error, not a real new event. Treat the Equinix nuclear picture as current through 2025-08-14 until a primary-source update surfaces.

**Governance signal, 2025-11-04:** Equinix appointed **Rebecca Kujawa** to its board of directors — ex-President & CEO of NextEra Energy Resources (managed $15–20B/year in capital investment across renewables, storage, gas, nuclear, and transmission), and Chairperson of **Nuclear Electric Insurers Limited (NEIL)**. A board-level, not procurement-desk, hire, but the clearest named-person signal of Equinix formalizing nuclear expertise at the top of the company, three months after the nuclear-deal bundle. [https://newsroom.equinix.com/2025-11-04-Rebecca-Kujawa-Appointed-to-Equinix-Board-of-Directors; Yahoo Finance]

**Pitch's use of this section:** Equinix is proof the microreactor-preorder-with-deposit instrument is real and already executed by a data-center operator — not a hypothetical structure Antares would have to invent. The Radiant Kaleidos spec (1.2 MWe, transportable, TRISO/HTGR) sits close enough to the R1 that "we're the second mover on exactly what Equinix already bought from a competitor" is a defensible pitch line. The Kujawa board appointment shows the buying signal reaches governance, not just facilities staff — useful for identifying who to approach first at a target account (EVP Global Operations / Chief Procurement Officer tier, per Abdel and SVP/CPO Ali Ruckteschler, with board-level nuclear literacy now in place behind them).

## 3. Backup/resilience generation buyers beyond data centers

Framed by class, since named individual operators piloting nuclear-specific backup are scarce; class-level funding and precedent signals are not.

- **State-funded resilience programs already target exactly these buyer classes, at billion-dollar scale, today — with non-nuclear technology.** Texas approved the **Texas Backup Power Package Program, $1.8 billion, announced 2025-06-11**, administered through the Texas Energy Fund, explicitly funding on-site backup (currently solar, battery storage, and gas generation) at **hospitals, water treatment plants, nursing homes, assisted-living centers, and police/fire stations**. [University of Houston Energy Innovation, citing the June 2025 announcement] Colorado has run a smaller parallel program since 2022, $15.4M across 28 microgrid grants, and DOE's Community Microgrid Assistance Partnership added $8M+ in similar funding. None of these programs name nuclear as an eligible technology yet — the pitch angle is that the budget line and buyer class already exist; a microreactor is a technology substitution into live procurement, not a request for new dollars. Notably, Texas is also Antares's own first-delivery state (JBSA).
- **Telecom has already made one non-diesel backup transition at thousands of sites — real precedent for a second one.** More than 3,000 fuel-cell backup systems are already installed at U.S. cellular sites (Sprint, T-Mobile, Verizon, AT&T); Verizon macro sites carry up to 8 hours of battery plus diesel generators sized for 24–72 hours; AT&T's equivalent figure is 4–72 hours depending on site type. No nuclear-specific telecom signal was found, but the fleet size and the fact the industry already swapped part of its diesel backup for fuel cells is a direct precedent for a microreactor pitch. [Plug Power; Light Reading; industry backup-power vendor pages]
- **Ports: one named, dated project.** The **Port of Long Beach microgrid** — built to back up the Port's Joint Command and Control Center (JCCC) emergency-response facility, currently diesel-dependent — had interconnection, cybersecurity testing, and commissioning expected in Q2 2025. The project's own framing: a single grid outage "could result in millions of dollars per day of damage" to the port economy. [energizeinnovation.fund / CEC Port of Long Beach microgrid project page]
- **Airports: signal is federal/defense, not civilian.** Oklo was selected to design, build, own, and operate an Aurora microreactor supplying both power and heat at **Eielson Air Force Base, Alaska**; the U.S. Army identified **nine installations** as candidate sites for a microreactor demonstration in **November 2025**. Antares's own ANPI/JBSA thread is the same buyer class (see `antares-facts.md`, fact 7) — not repeated here. The one civilian-airport resilience project surfaced (Fairchild International Airport, Port Angeles, WA, a federal disaster-relief staging area) is being built on solar/storage, not nuclear.
- **Financial exchanges: no signal found.** Two targeted searches (a combined query and a follow-up) surfaced no RFP, resilience investment, or pilot program tying a stock exchange, clearing house, or trading venue to backup-power resilience of any kind, nuclear or otherwise. This class is absent from the evidence base, not weakly evidenced — worth flagging to the pitch team as an open question rather than a soft "maybe."

**Pitch's use of this section:** the strongest class-level argument is Texas's own $1.8B program — it proves these exact buyer types (hospitals, water utilities, emergency services) already have live, funded backup-power budgets sized in the billions, currently spent on solar/battery/gas. The telecom fuel-cell fleet proves an entire regulated industry has already substituted one backup technology for diesel at thousands of sites without an existential fight. Neither data point mentions nuclear yet — that's the opening, not a rebuttal.

## 4. Remote and harsh-environment operations

**Oil & gas and mining formed a joint nuclear-demand consortium — the single strongest named-company signal in this whole research pass.** The **Industrial Advanced Nuclear™ Consortium (IANC)**, launched by The Open Group on **2025-09-02** (BusinessWire; opengroup.org), has seven founding members: **ExxonMobil, Shell, Chevron, ConocoPhillips** (oil & gas), **Rio Tinto, Freeport-McMoRan** (mining), and **Nucor** (steel). Per World Nuclear News's description of the consortium's own materials, its named use cases are exactly the ones DeWahl flagged in the antares-facts.md podcast quote: **"diesel replacement for remote well pads and offshore operations," floating offshore platforms, heat/power for refineries and LNG facilities, and mining/upstream operations** — explicitly covering both small *and* micro modular reactors (SMRs/MMRs), not just utility-scale designs. ExxonMobil's Mohan Kalyanaraman, quoted: the goal is "making nuclear a viable option for industrial projects by 2030." [businesswire.com, 2025-09-02; opengroup.org/IANC; World Nuclear News description, dated by its own byline 2026-06-29]
- **Tempering signal, same sector:** a MINING.COM analysis (search-surfaced only; the direct fetch returned HTTP 403, so treat as press-reported, not independently verified) reports a mining-industry survey finding **fewer than 10% of miners actively considering SMRs, versus more than 70% favoring wind/solar** — and frames most SMR designs as oversized (300 MW-class) for a standalone mine, versus microreactors in the 1–20 MW range, "closer to mine-site scale," which it describes as still early-commercialization. This is the honest state of the sector: a real consortium exists at the top, but ground-level mining procurement hasn't moved toward nuclear yet — and when it does, the size class it needs is the R1's, not a 300 MW SMR's.
- **Alaska: the most concrete remote-microgrid signal found.** Governor Mike Dunleavy has publicly backed microreactors for diesel-dependent rural villages, and the state has passed microreactor-specific siting/regulatory provisions (gov.alaska.gov). On **2026-07-30**, NuCube Energy Inc. and the Alaska Fund signed an MOU to form **NuAlaska**, a joint initiative to develop, finance, own, and operate advanced nuclear projects in the state (via The Alaska Story). Separately and already federal (see `antares-facts.md`): Oklo is contracted to design/build/own/operate an Aurora microreactor at Eielson AFB, Alaska, and has a supply deal with Siemens Energy.
- **Puerto Rico: real but slow-moving.** The **Nuclear Alternative Project (NAP)**, founded 2016 by Puerto Rican nuclear-industry engineers, ran a DOE-sponsored feasibility study (completed 2020) explicitly covering **both microreactors and SMRs** for the island, concluding they could fit a distributed-microgrid rebuild strategy adopted after Hurricane Maria (2017); NAP moved to site-analysis phase by 2021 (two candidate sites: a north-coast industrial hub and the former Roosevelt Roads Naval Station). Freshest signal: in 2025, the University of Puerto Rico–Mayagüez's ANS student chapter testified before the Puerto Rico House in support of **Proyecto de la Cámara 1092**, a bill directing the territory to evaluate SMR deployment. No construction commitment exists yet. [World Nuclear News, 2021-09-09 for the site-analysis stage; 2025 legislative activity via ANS/search coverage]
- **Desalination and Antarctic stations: real interest, weak/no microreactor-scale signal.** NuScale presented DOE/PNNL-partnered desalination research (March 2025, World Petrochemical Conference) — but its 77 MW Power Module is SMR-, not microreactor-, scale. Antarctic nuclear power has one historical precedent (a reactor operated at McMurdo Station for roughly a decade, 1962–1972) and one **notional, student-originated proposal** for Chile's Escudero station pairing renewables with a microreactor — flagged explicitly as low-confidence, not a procurement signal, since the only source found frames it as a 17-year-old's design proposal, not an institutional program.

**Pitch's use of this section:** the IANC is the strongest evidence in this entire document that named, credit-worthy industrial buyers — not just hyperscalers — are organizing demand for exactly the R1's use case (diesel displacement, remote/offshore, sub-SMR scale) right now, with a public 2030 viability target from ExxonMobil itself. Alaska is the most actionable near-term geography: state political will, new regulation, and a freshly signed development MOU (NuAlaska, five weeks before this research) all point the same direction.

## 5. "Doesn't need an SMR yet" loads

**The size-class split is a real, named industry distinction, not a framing this pitch has to invent.** Multiple sources converge on the same rough boundary: **SMRs run up to roughly 300 MWe** (Mordor Intelligence, Precedence Research, and the IANC/mining framing in Section 4 all use this ceiling); **microreactors are generally defined as under 10 MWe**, with most commercial designs (Antares R1, Radiant Kaleidos, Project Pele at 1.5 MWe) clustered at roughly 0.1–1.5 MWe per unit. A single 300 MW SMR module is sized for "300,000 homes, a large data-center campus, or a cluster of industrial facilities" — describing a different buyer than a standalone edge site.
- **Named analyst/industry framing, from a competitor's own leadership:** NANO Nuclear Energy CEO James Walker and Founder/Executive Chairman Jay Jiang Yu, on a Data Center Frontier podcast (published **2023-12-19** — framing is dated but the market split it describes is the current industry consensus, corroborated by the newer sources above): microreactors target "mine sites, charging stations, remote habitations, disaster relief areas, military bases" and smaller/remote data centers "principally" running on diesel today, while SMRs (Walker cites ~100 MW-class) serve "enormous data centers and AI." Walker: "It's smaller, it'll be cheaper, it'll be safer, it'll be more deployable, you'll have far more economies of scale." A specific, previously unseen-in-this-research buyer type from Yu: **"Bitcoin mining companies want to power data centers in cold environments like Alaska"** — reinforcing the Alaska/cold-climate thread from Section 4 with a named crypto/compute angle rather than only hyperscaler AI.
- **0.5–5 MW critical-load buyer types, assembled from this and the prior three sections (no single source lists all of these together):** edge/regional data centers below hyperscaler campus scale; telecom central offices and switching sites (Section 3 — currently diesel/fuel-cell backed at the sub-1MW-per-site level); industrial and manufacturing sites too small to justify a dedicated SMR (the IANC's own "energy-intensive manufacturing" category, Section 4); remote mine sites (1–20 MW per the MINING.COM framing, Section 4); EV-charging plazas (named directly by the market-segmentation sources above, no specific operator identified); defense primes' own facilities — a plausible category by analogy to the Air Force/Army microreactor programs already documented in `antares-facts.md`, but **no specific defense-contractor-facility signal was found** in this research pass, so it is listed as a hypothesis, not a verified lead.

**Pitch's use of this section:** this is the direct rebuttal to "why not just wait for a cheaper SMR" — the buyer classes above have loads an order of magnitude below a 300 MW SMR's minimum efficient scale, so a microreactor isn't a stepping-stone technology for them, it's the only right-sized option that exists today.

## 6. International: which countries first for a U.S. microreactor company

All candidates below sit inside long-standing U.S. peaceful-nuclear-cooperation frameworks (bilateral agreements for the UK, Japan, and South Korea; the EURATOM-wide U.S. agreement covering Poland, Romania, and Czechia) — this section ranks them by which have a *microreactor- or SMR-specific program actually in motion*, not just legal eligibility.

1. **United Kingdom — strongest candidate.** Three independent signals converge: (a) the **US-UK "Tech Prosperity Deal"** restarted in **February 2026** after a December-2025 pause, explicitly targeting civil-nuclear cooperation — shared safety assessments, coordinated licensing, a goal of ~2-year design reviews and 1-year site licensing (Power Magazine; Herbert Smith Freehills Kramer); (b) **Rolls-Royce SMR** was named the UK government's preferred bidder for the country's first domestic SMR fleet in 2025; (c) most directly relevant to Antares's own class: competitor **Last Energy's PWR-20 microreactor** (20 MWe) completed a **Preliminary Design Review by the UK's Office for Nuclear Regulation in July 2025**, targeting a named commercial customer — **DP World's Thames Freeport facility** — online by **2030**. A U.S. microreactor company gets a friendly regulator actively fast-tracking design review, a live government-to-government deal covering exactly this technology, and proof a British port operator will already sign for microreactor power.
2. **Romania — most construction-advanced.** U.S. cooperation on SMRs there dates to a **2019 MOU** (Nuclearelectrica + NuScale), a **2021** teaming agreement for a NuScale VOYGR-6 plant, and — the strongest milestone in this entire international section — a **Final Investment Decision approved in February 2026** for six NuScale SMRs, moving the relationship from agreement to funded construction. [Neutron Bytes, 2026-02-13; euronuclear.org]
3. **Czech Republic — regulator and industrial base both moving.** Rolls-Royce SMR signed MOUs with Czech utility **ČEZ** and the Czech Ministry of Industry and Trade (dated by source **2026-07-02**) to prepare two additional SMR sites (Tušimice, Dětmarovice) toward a stated 3 GW ambition, plus a separate MOU with **Škoda JS** to manufacture SMR components for a global fleet — a signal Czechia wants to be a supply-chain partner, not just a buyer. [NucNet]
4. **Japan + South Korea, as a paired opportunity via a fresh U.S. government channel.** On **2026-07-07**, at the NATO Summit in Ankara, the U.S. (Secretary of State Rubio), Japan (FM Motegi), and South Korea (FM Cho Hyun) signed a **trilateral Memorandum of Cooperation on SMR deployment in third countries** — an explicit division of labor (U.S. reactor design, Japan heavy manufacturing, Korea construction speed/supply chain), aimed first at the Indo-Pacific, with talks reportedly already underway with one unnamed Indo-Pacific country. [state.gov release — title/date corroborated by multiple press outlets (UPI, Korea Times, nuclear-news.net, U.S. Embassy Beijing mirror); direct fetch of the state.gov page failed on encoding, so treat as press-corroborated, not independently read in full] Both governments have decades-standing nuclear-cooperation relationships with the U.S. — this MOC is new government machinery a microreactor company could plug into for export support, not a from-scratch relationship.
5. **Poland — earlier-stage, worth tracking.** Polish miner **KGHM** signed an MoU with Romania's Nuclearelectrica to cooperate on SMR development and is separately pursuing a NuScale VOYGR deployment "as early as 2029." Less advanced than Romania or Czechia, but a second concrete Central European thread, and KGHM's own identity as a mining company pursuing nuclear power echoes the IANC signal in Section 4.

**NATO/allied-base energy resilience — one concrete, if dated, signal.** Microreactor company **Last Energy** partnered with **NATO's Energy Security Centre of Excellence (ENSEC COE, Vilnius, Lithuania)** on **2024-06-03** — joint research on microreactor applications for NATO military installations, with Last Energy as ENSEC COE's first-ever nuclear-industry advisor. No specific base or deployment timeline was attached at signing. [World Nuclear News, 2024-06-03] This predates the Army's own November-2025 Janus nine-base plan (see `antares-facts.md`); trade press (AFCEA) frames a working U.S. Army microreactor model as "an exportable template" for NATO allies worried about energy coercion — a framing, not yet a program.

**Not found:** a Ukraine-reconstruction-specific nuclear/microreactor signal, and Australia/AUKUS civil (non-naval) microreactor cooperation. The one AUKUS nuclear agreement located (in force **2025-01-17**) governs *naval reactor* material and technology transfer among the U.S./UK/Australia — a different legal track from civil microreactor exports, and not used here as a civil-power signal.

**Pitch's use of this section:** the UK is the strongest first-country case — a fast-moving friendly regulator, an active bilateral nuclear deal, and a directly comparable competitor microreactor already through preliminary design review with a named commercial (port) customer. Romania is the best "the U.S. government machinery already works" proof point, since it's the one relationship that has actually reached a funded construction decision.
