I tightened the ranges to annual-average electrical demand. Full steelmaking, large LNG plants and standalone irrigation pumping have been removed. Large data centers, semiconductor fabs and similar sites remain only where a defined 1–20 MW power block is useful.

### 1. Compute

Regional data centers (1–10 MW); company-owned data centers (1–5 MW); shared data centers (5–50 MW); supercomputing centers (10–50 MW); AI and very large cloud data centers (50–500+ MW total, using a 1–20 MW dedicated block); and dedicated baseload and outage-protection blocks within larger data-center campuses (1–20 MW).

### 2. Mining

Large pozzolan mines with onsite crushing, grinding or drying (1–5 MW); remote lithium and rare-earth mines with onsite processing (5–20 MW); and remote copper and other large mines (12–25 MW for a representative site, with larger mines using a 5–20 MW partial-power block).

### 3. Electric Utilities

Individual remote grids with sufficient year-round demand (1–10 MW); regional grids serving several communities (5–20 MW); replacements or partial replacements for older diesel and coal generation (1–20 MW); power for new mines, factories, data centers and communities before major grid upgrades (1–20 MW); and staged additions for future load growth (1–20 MW).

### 4. Manufacturing

Steel rolling and finishing plants, excluding full steelmaking mills (5–20 MW); cement plants (7–25 MW); lime plants (2–8 MW); chemical plants (5–50 MW, with a 5–20 MW block at larger plants); fertilizer plants (5–30 MW, with a 5–20 MW block at larger plants); hydrogen and low-carbon fuel plants (5–20 MW per production block); pulp and paper mills (10–40 MW, with partial supply above 20 MW); large integrated sawmills and wood-products plants (1–8 MW); semiconductor fabs (30–150+ MW total, using a 5–20 MW dedicated block); battery-cell plants (20–100+ MW total, using a 5–20 MW block); battery-material plants (5–30 MW); and battery-recycling plants (2–15 MW).

### 5. Oil & Gas

Remote oil fields and production hubs (5–20 MW); offshore platforms (10–50 MW total, using a 5–20 MW block at larger platforms); pipeline pumping and electric compression stations (5–20 MW per station); gas-processing plants (5–20 MW); smaller liquefied-natural-gas plants and receiving terminals (5–20 MW); smaller refineries (5–20 MW); and dedicated electrical blocks at larger refineries (5–20 MW).

### 6. Transportation

Large Amazon, Walmart and similar fulfillment and distribution centers (1–3 MW normally; 2–10 MW with heavy automation or fleet charging); refrigerated distribution centers (1–5 MW); cargo-port electrical systems combining terminal operations and ship plug-in power (5–20 MW average, with higher short-term peaks); major passenger airports (10–30 MW average); major air-cargo campuses (3–15 MW); and large rail, truck and fleet-charging hubs (1–10 MW average, with peaks of 5–30 MW).

### 7. Civic Infrastructure

Large individual hospitals (2–8 MW); major medical campuses (10–40 MW); large prison complexes (1–3 MW); major universities (5–30 MW); national laboratories (10–50+ MW, using a 5–20 MW block at larger sites); large campus electrical systems (5–30 MW); regional drinking-water treatment and pumping systems (1–10 MW); major city wastewater plants (1–15 MW); and large seawater desalination plants (5–20 MW).

### 8. Agriculture & Food

Large lighted greenhouse campuses (1–8 MW); large indoor-growing facilities (1–10 MW); large land-based aquaculture operations (1–5 MW); remote seafood-processing plants connected to a year-round community grid (1–5 MW during processing); very large meat, dairy and frozen-food plants (2–15 MW); large grain and oilseed mills (5–20 MW); and integrated agricultural campuses combining growing, processing, refrigeration and water pumping (2–20 MW).

These are planning bands rather than guaranteed averages; final sizing would require at least one year of hourly site-load data. The principal calibration sources were the [LBNL data-center report](https://eta-publications.lbl.gov/sites/default/files/2024-12/lbnl-2024-united-states-data-center-energy-usage-report.pdf?stream=), [CNL/OPG remote-mine study](https://www.opg.com/documents/smr-economic-feasibility-and-cost-benefit-study-for-remote-mining/), [Port of Seattle airport energy data](https://www.portseattle.org/sites/default/files/2026-05/2024%20AV%20Scope%2012_Web_v3.pdf) and [DOE desalination study](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2017/12/f46/Seawater_desalination_bandwidth_study_2017.pdf).
